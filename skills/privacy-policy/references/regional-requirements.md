# What each region and store requires a privacy policy to say

Include a region's items when the product has users there; where the operator is based matters less, though the
operator's home law always applies too. When the audience is "everywhere", the EU and California items together
cover most of what other laws ask for. These are checklists to make sure nothing is missed, not legal advice.

## EU and EEA (GDPR), UK (UK GDPR)

GDPR Articles 13 and 14 list what users must be told:

- The controller's identity and contact details; the EU or UK representative when the operator is outside and
  must appoint one (Article 27); the data protection officer's contact if one is appointed.
- Each purpose and its legal basis under Article 6: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public
  task or legitimate interests. When relying on legitimate interests, name the interest.
- The recipients or categories of recipients (processors such as hosting, analytics, email).
- Transfers outside the EEA or UK and the safeguard used: an adequacy decision (for US providers certified under
  the EU–US Data Privacy Framework) or Standard Contractual Clauses.
- How long each kind of data is kept, or the criteria that decide it.
- The rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection (including an absolute
  right to object to direct marketing), the right to withdraw consent at any time, and the right to complain to a
  supervisory authority (in the UK, the ICO).
- Whether providing the data is a legal or contractual requirement, and what happens if the user doesn't.
- Automated decision-making, including profiling, with meaningful information about the logic, when decisions
  with legal or similarly significant effects are made that way.
- When data comes from someone other than the user (Article 14): the source and the categories of data.

Children: for online services offered directly to children, consent needs a parent's authorisation under 16, which
member states may lower to 13 (the UK uses 13). Cookies and similar trackers need consent under the ePrivacy rules
unless strictly necessary; see the cookie-policy skill.

## United States

**California, CCPA as amended by the CPRA.** It applies to for-profit businesses with annual gross revenue above
the threshold (USD 25 million, adjusted for inflation), or that buy, sell or share the personal information of
100,000 or more consumers or households a year, or that make half or more of their revenue from selling or sharing
it. Smaller businesses often include the section anyway. The policy must give:

- the categories of personal information collected in the past 12 months, their sources, and the business or
  commercial purposes;
- the categories disclosed for a business purpose and the categories of recipients;
- whether personal information was sold or shared in the past 12 months (sharing means for cross-context
  behavioral advertising, which most ad and retargeting pixels do);
- how long each category is kept;
- any use of sensitive personal information;
- the rights to know, delete, correct, opt out of sale and sharing, and limit the use of sensitive personal
  information, and the right not to be discriminated against for using them;
- how to submit requests (usually two ways, such as an email address and a web form), how requests are verified,
  and how an authorized agent can act for a consumer;
- a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link when data is sold or shared, and that opt-out preference
  signals such as Global Privacy Control are honored;
- an update at least every 12 months, with the date.

**California, CalOPPA.** It applies to any site or online service that collects personally identifiable
information from California residents, with no size threshold. The policy must name the categories collected and
the third parties they're shared with, explain how users can review and request changes (if that's offered) and
how they're told about policy changes, give the effective date, say how the service responds to browser "Do Not
Track" signals, and say whether third parties may collect information about users' activity over time and across
sites.

**Other states** (Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Texas, Oregon and a growing list) give similar rights:
access, correction, deletion, portability, and opting out of targeted advertising, sale and profiling, plus a way
to appeal a refused request. One "Your U.S. state privacy rights" section with an appeal process covers them.

**Children, COPPA.** A service directed to children under 13, or that knowingly collects their data, needs a
specific notice and verifiable parental consent, which goes beyond the policy; tell the user. Otherwise say the
service isn't directed to children under 13 and that such data is deleted when discovered.

## Turkey (KVKK)

Law No. 6698 on the Protection of Personal Data. The duty to inform (Article 10) requires:

- the identity of the data controller (veri sorumlusu) and of its representative, if any;
- the purposes of processing;
- to whom and for what purposes the data may be transferred;
- the method and legal ground of collection;
- the data subject's rights under Article 11: to learn whether their data is processed and request information
  about it, to learn the purpose and whether it's used accordingly, to know the third parties it's transferred to
  in Turkey or abroad, to ask for correction and for deletion or destruction, to have those notified to the third
  parties, to object to a result against them arising solely from automated analysis, and to claim compensation
  for damage from unlawful processing.

Requests go to the controller in writing, by registered email (KEP), with a secure electronic or mobile signature,
or from an email address the user has already given the controller; the controller answers within 30 days.
Explicit consent (açık rıza), where needed, is asked separately and must not be bundled into the notice or into
acceptance of the terms. Transfers abroad, since the amendment by Law No. 7499 in force from 1 June 2024, rest on
an adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards such as the Board's standard contracts (which must be notified to the
Authority within five business days of signing), or the limited exceptions for occasional transfers.

Turkish companies usually publish a separate "KVKK Aydınlatma Metni". When users are in Turkey, either add a KVKK
section in Turkish or offer to publish the aydınlatma metni as its own document (document type "Other").

## Brazil (LGPD)

Close to GDPR: the controller's identity and contact, the data protection officer's (encarregado) contact, the
purposes and legal bases, who data is shared with and why, international transfers, retention, and the rights in
Article 18 (confirmation, access, correction, anonymization, blocking or deletion, portability, information about
sharing, information about refusing consent and its consequences, revoking consent), plus the right to petition
the ANPD.

## Canada (PIPEDA, Quebec Law 25)

PIPEDA: who is accountable for privacy (a name or title and contact), the purposes identified before or at
collection, meaningful consent, how to access and correct personal information, and how to complain (to the
organization, then to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner). Quebec's Law 25 adds the title and contact details
of the person in charge of the protection of personal information, published on the site, and disclosure of any
technology that identifies, locates or profiles users, with how to turn it on.

## Australia (Privacy Act)

For organizations it covers (mainly those with annual turnover above AUD 3 million, plus some smaller ones), the
policy follows Australian Privacy Principle 1: the kinds of information collected and held, how and why, how to
access and correct it, how to complain and how complaints are handled, and whether information is likely to be
disclosed overseas and, where practicable, to which countries.

## App stores and extension stores

**Apple App Store.** Every app needs a privacy policy URL in App Store Connect, and the policy must be reachable
from inside the app. It must say what data the app collects, how, and for what, confirm that third parties
receiving it give equal protection, and explain retention and deletion and how users can revoke consent or ask
for deletion. The App Privacy details ("privacy nutrition labels") must match it. Apps that let users create an
account must let them start deleting it from within the app (App Review Guideline 5.1.1(v)). Tracking across
other companies' apps and sites needs the App Tracking Transparency prompt.

**Google Play.** A privacy policy link is required in Play Console and inside the app, on a public, active URL
that isn't geofenced and isn't a PDF, labelled as a privacy policy. It must say what user data the app accesses,
collects, uses and shares and with whom, give a privacy contact, explain secure handling, and give the retention
and deletion policy. The Data safety form must agree with it. Apps that let users create an account must offer
account deletion inside the app and through a web link that works without reinstalling the app.

**Chrome Web Store.** An extension that handles user data needs a privacy policy, and the privacy practices
declared in the Developer Dashboard must match it, including the Limited Use statement where it applies.
